Verdict: GO for the shelving, Thursday

The operator's first line is "Shelving units for sale." Later: *"Metal

restaurant shelves, plastic too. Storage cabinets."*

Metal restaurant shelving is the NSF wire type that taps apart with a rubber

mallet and lies flat in the Bolt — the exact thing bought at Leon's. A used bay

is $50-80 replacement; at estate-sale prices it should be $15-30.

Also named: hand tools, power tools, a vintage bike, a metal wine rack, jewelry

and watches, albums, Amish bed set, vintage sport team jackets.

What I could NOT verify, and why

My photo sample missed the shelving entirely. I pulled 20 of 184 photos on a

1-in-9 stride and got archery accessories, shoeboxes and kitchenware. The

shelving is named in the copy but not in the slice I looked at.

Two causes, both worth recording:

1. Stride sampling is wrong for a targeted search. It characterises a room;

it does not find a specific object. For "does this sale have shelving", the

choices are pull the whole gallery or go look.

2. The operator says "More photos Tuesday" — the listing may still have been

filling when I scouted it.

So: the shelving is a claim in the description, not something I have seen.

How to work it

1. Call or message Castle Estate Sales first. Ask two questions: how many

shelving units, and are they the wire knock-down type or welded? That is a

two-minute call that decides whether the 7.3 miles is worth it.

2. If you go: the two hard rules still apply — **must disassemble with a drill

and a mallet, and every part must fit the Bolt.** Welded racks and anything

over ~74 in of post fail.

3. Walk the tools while you are there. "Power tools" in estate-sale copy is

usually corded consumer gear, but Milwaukee and Makita turn up.

4. Lower priority than Warren (records, 2.1 mi) if you only do one.
