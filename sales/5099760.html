The Strat — what to check before you offer anything

This is the only item here with real upside and real downside. On the wall it

is just a white Strat with scribbles.

1. Read the signatures. Photograph them straight-on and legibly. Unknown or

unreadable scrawls = decorated guitar, $80-150.

2. What guitar is it? Headstock decal: Fender USA, Fender Mexico, Squier?

A Squier body with signatures is worth the signatures only.

3. Is there a COA, photo, or story? Unauthenticated band signatures sell at

a steep discount, exactly like the Chelios jersey — the difference there was

a punched, dated COA from a real venue.

4. Only then price it. If it is a documented multi-signature piece from a

named act, $400-1,200. If it is unauthenticated, treat it as a $120 guitar

with decoration and bid accordingly.

How to work the records — the actual plan

At thousands of LPs you cannot cherry-pick in an hour, and flipping every

sleeve is a full day. Two options:

A. Dig (2-3 hours). Flip for LABELS not titles: Blue Note, Prestige,

Impulse, Riverside, Verve, Savoy, Atlantic/ATCO, Motown/Tamla, Stax. Check the

label address and deep groove the way we do on the Neils sheets. Era reads

1960s-heavy (ATCO, MGM, Wand, Columbia spines), which is the right era.

B. Negotiate a section. "All prices negotiable" plus thousands of records

plus a dealer clearing stock = ask what a whole shelf bay costs. At $1-2/record

on a 400-record bay, one Blue Note NY USA original pays for the bay.

Do A first for an hour, then B with what you have learned.
